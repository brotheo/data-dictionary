Learning objectives

  • Filter rows with df.loc[]; combine conditions with & and |
  • Sort with sort_values() and select top results with head()
  • Summarise groups with groupby() and calculate a correlation
  • Write reusable functions that take a DataFrame as a parameter

Success criteria

  • Completes the three core worksheets with output matching the key
  • Explains why and fails between pandas conditions
  • States that correlation does not prove cause
  • Spots that Year and Subject are confounded in this dataset

Lesson 4 · Reusable toolkit (Boss level)

def filter_by_score(dataframe, minimum_score):
    result = dataframe.loc[dataframe["Score"] > minimum_score]
    return result

def get_top_students(dataframe, number_of_students):
    result = dataframe.sort_values("Score", ascending=False)
    return result.head(number_of_students)

def calculate_average_score(dataframe):
    average = dataframe["Score"].mean()
    return round(average, 2)

print("Students scoring above 80:")
print(filter_by_score(df, 80)[["Name", "Subject", "Score"]])
print("\nTop five students:")
print(get_top_students(df, 5)[["Name", "Subject", "Score"]])
print("\nAverage student score:")
print(calculate_average_score(df))
filter_by_score(df, 80) → 10 rows (same as Lesson 1 Task 1) get_top_students(df, 5) → Zara 96, George 95, Bella 92, Priya 91, Lily 90 calculate_average_score(df) → 77.5 Extra tests: filter_by_score(df, 95) → 1 row (Zara) · Science average → 74.67

Model findings (Task 5)

  1. Subjects: Mathematics had the highest average (84.0), but every Maths student is in Year 11, so subject and year group can't be separated.
  2. Study hours: strong positive association, r = 0.96; the 9+ hours group averaged 89.33 vs 56.25 for 1–4 hours.
  3. Attendance: 90–100% attendance averaged 86.75; the three students below 80% averaged 54.0. Attendance vs score r = 0.99.
  4. Conclusion: strong associations, not proof of cause; 20 fictional records; next step is data with every year group in every subject.

Watch for functions that use df inside instead of the dataframe parameter. They still "work" on this data but aren't reusable; deduct in the rubric.

Round 4 · Mystery files (60 XP each)

  1. "Maths is the easiest exam." Answer: we can't tell. Every Maths student is in Year 11 and every Year 11 student took Maths, so subject and year group are confounded.
  2. "If I study 15 hours I'll get 96." Answer: wrong. Correlation shows variables move together in this data; attendance (r = 0.99) and unmeasured factors move with them too.

Cleaning worksheet solution

df = raw.drop_duplicates(subset="Order_ID").copy()

df["Item"] = df["Item"].str.title()

valid_item = df["Item"].isin(prices.keys())
valid_qty  = df["Quantity"].between(1, 10)
valid_year = df["Year_Group"].isin([9, 10, 11])
df = df.loc[valid_item & valid_qty & valid_year].copy()

df["Price"]    = df["Item"].map(prices)
df["Category"] = df["Item"].map(categories)
df["Revenue"]  = df["Price"] * df["Quantity"]

print("Clean rows:", len(df))   # 20

The worksheet line is df = raw.______; adding .copy() is good practice and avoids a pandas warning in some versions. Accept list(prices) or prices itself inside isin(). Watch for .str.capitalize(), which turns "Fruit Cup" into "Fruit cup" and silently removes 2 more rows (18 clean rows).
